Low-rank adapters (LoRA) fine-tuned from one base model are often merged into
a single model. We show that one detail, almost never reported, decides
whether a whole class of merges works: whether the adapters started from one
random draw of the LoRA $A$ factor or from independent draws. One training
seed, or the defaults of Unsloth and LLaMA-Factory, gives a shared draw. The
task subspaces then collapse: their median principal cosine exceeds 0.99,
against 0.05 for independent draws, on four base models. The initialisation
seed alone decides this. Training every task on from one adapter, or
initialising with PiSSA, collapses a cohort without a shared seed. On
collapsed cohorts, WUDI-Merging, a published data-free method run as its
authors run it, leaves a worst-task loss excess 4.7 to 47.6 times larger than
on independent cohorts. On GSM8K it scores 0.03 against 0.70 on Llama-3.1-8B.
A data-free form of RegMean and our closed-form encoder fail too. All three
solve a least-squares problem built from the adapters alone. Averaging and
sparsifying merges, and solvers built from data, are unaffected. A ridge of
0.01 recovers most of the lost GSM8K accuracy on three of four bases;
RegMean's shipped regulariser is too weak to do so. A first-order theory
matches the conditioning within 0.10\% and predicts it on eight cohorts
trained after the theory was fixed. An audit that reads the adapter files in
a CPU minute finds the collapse in at least 18 of 45 public LoRA cohorts.
Every confirmatory test was pre-registered, and we report the predictions
that failed.
